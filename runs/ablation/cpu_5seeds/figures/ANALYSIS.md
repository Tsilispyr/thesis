# Ablation Matrix Figures (cpu_5seeds) -- Figure-by-Figure Analysis

**Purpose of this document**: the figure-level counterpart to `runs/ablation/cpu_run1/figures/ANALYSIS.md`, at the same level of detail, for the 5-seed extension. It does not repeat `runs/ablation/cpu_5seeds/ANALYSIS.md`'s tables (the point-estimate/CI comparison against `cpu_run1`, the newly-clean pairwise wins, the coefficient-of-variation update, the LSTM training-dynamics finding) -- it explains what the pictures behind those numbers actually look like, and where they visibly differ from `cpu_run1`'s own figure set now that 2 more seeds are in the mix. All numbers below come from `ai_backend/models/history_*.json`, `runs/ablation/cpu_5seeds/results.json`, and this folder's own `trajectory_eval_ablation.json`.

## The representative-seed rule, restated for 5 seeds

Same rule as `cpu_run1`, unchanged: for the four figures that can only show one trajectory per model (`trajectory_3d.png`, `trajectory_xy.png`, `trajectory_xz.png`, `error_over_time.png`), each stochastic model's representative seed is the one whose final chained error is the statistical **median** of all 5 seeds (`visualize_ablation.py::median_seed_index()`), not the best or the worst. With 5 seeds instead of 3, the median is the true middle value (3rd of 5, sorted), not an average of two candidates:

| Model | Representative seed | Final err [m] (this seed) | All 5 seeds' final err [m] |
|---|---|---|---|
| LSTM | seed 1 | 29.48 | 30.01, **29.48**, 35.64, 28.46, 24.78 |
| SSL-LSTM | seed 4 | 33.46 | 36.87, 37.51, 31.92, 33.08, **33.46** |
| Transformer | seed 2 | 7.15 | 14.17, 3.22, **7.15**, 15.04, 5.94 |
| SSL-Transformer | seed 0 | 13.22 | **13.22**, 11.15, 19.80, 9.41, 16.39 |
| RandomForest | seed 1 | 26.19 | 27.27, **26.19**, 26.59, 26.13, 25.92 |
| GBT | seed 4 | 18.29 | 18.06, 20.87, 17.27, 18.31, **18.29** |

Four of the six representative seeds changed from `cpu_run1`'s table (LSTM was seed 0, is now seed 1; SSL-Transformer stayed seed 0; SSL-LSTM was seed 0, is now seed 4; RandomForest was seed 2, is now seed 1; GBT was seed 0, is now seed 4; only Transformer stayed seed 2) -- an expected, mechanical consequence of the median shifting as two more data points join the sorted list, not a sign of anything unstable in the selection rule itself. Worth noting explicitly: the two new seeds (3 and 4) did not simply cluster near the existing three's values -- seed 3's Transformer run (15.04 m) is the new *worst* Transformer seed of all 5, and seed 4's LSTM run (24.78 m) is the new *best* LSTM seed of all 5 (see below) -- both extremes came from the new data, which is exactly why the wider seed set changes some representative-seed choices and tightens the CI table at the same time.

## Training curves -- what's different from `cpu_run1` now that 2 more seeds are visible

**`train_lstm.png`**: this is the one training-curve figure that looks *qualitatively* different from its `cpu_run1` counterpart, not just more crowded. The original 3-seed version showed three near-identical, near-interleaved overfitting curves, all plateauing within 6-8 epochs. With seeds 3 and 4 added, the picture splits into two distinct groups: seeds 0-2 still plateau early (early-stopping at epochs 6-8, `best_epoch` 1-3), while **seed 3 runs to epoch 10 and seed 4 runs the entire 30-epoch budget without early-stopping at all** (`best_epoch=30`, still improving at the last epoch, `ai_backend/models/history_imu_norm_ablation_seed4.json`). Visually, seeds 3 and 4's solid train-loss lines (the lighter gray pair) keep descending well past where the other three flatten out, reaching a noticeably lower final training loss (~0.12 by epoch 30 vs. ~0.19-0.24 for the early-stopped seeds), and seed 4's dotted validation-loss line is the lowest of all five by the end. This is the direct visual evidence behind `runs/ablation/cpu_5seeds/ANALYSIS.md`'s training-dynamics finding, and behind `ai_backend/models/ANALYSIS.md`'s subsequent production-checkpoint update: this figure is what made the "seed 4 might not have actually finished converging" hypothesis visible in the first place, before it was tested directly (`ai_backend/continue_training_best_lstm.py`) and found to be a genuine, if narrow, case of a run reaching real convergence right at the edge of its epoch budget, not one still improving.

**`train_ssl_lstm_pretrain.png`**: unchanged in character from `cpu_run1` -- the pretext task remains clean and well-behaved across all 5 seeds now, train and validation loss tracking closely, converging to essentially the same place (best validation loss 0.1524-0.1552 across all 5, versus 0.1524-0.1542 across the original 3). The 2 new seeds do not disrupt this figure's story at all, consistent with `cpu_run1/figures/ANALYSIS.md`'s reading that the pretext task itself, not the fine-tuning stage, is where any instability would have to come from.

**`train_ssl_lstm_finetune.png`**: the SSL penalty documented at 3 seeds holds at 5 -- best validation loss now spans 0.7653-0.8176 across all 5 seeds, every one of them still worse than every one of the from-scratch LSTM's 5 seeds' range (0.6846-0.7475, overlapping at the boundary this time: SSL-LSTM's best seed, 0.7653, is worse than the from-scratch LSTM's worst seed, 0.7475, so the two 5-number sets no longer have a strict zero-overlap gap the way the 3-seed sets did, a small softening worth noting honestly even though the direction and the mean gap are unchanged).

**`train_transformer.png`**: still visibly the least consistent training curve in the set, but the specific shape of the instability is different with 5 seeds. At 3 seeds, the three validation-loss traces fanned out into three cleanly-separated, non-crossing bands. At 5 seeds, the picture is messier: most seeds converge into a fairly tight band by epoch 10-12 (roughly 0.33-0.39), but **seed 3's validation loss visibly turns upward after epoch 8-9**, climbing back to ~0.44 by epoch 14 rather than continuing to improve or plateau -- the only one of the five to reverse direction like this. This is the direct visual match to `runs/ablation/cpu_5seeds/ANALYSIS.md`'s decoupling finding: seed 3 has a mid-pack windowed loss (0.3550, better than seed 2's final 0.4049) but ends up with the *worst* chained-trajectory error of all 5 seeds (15.04 m) -- the rising validation-loss tail visible in this figure is consistent with (though does not by itself prove) a model that was starting to overfit in a way the windowed metric only partially captures before early stopping caught it.

**`train_ssl_transformer_pretrain.png`**: unchanged in character -- clean, low-loss, no overfitting, all 5 seeds converging to a similar place (best validation loss 0.0173-0.0198, versus 0.0172-0.0198 across the original 3).

**`train_ssl_transformer_finetune.png`**: best validation loss now spans 0.3430-0.3792 across 5 seeds (versus 0.3429-0.3792 across 3 -- the extremes barely moved, since the original min/max seeds are still seeds 1 and 0 respectively). The windowed-metric mean (0.362) remains marginally *better* than the from-scratch Transformer's (0.365, see `comparison_val_loss.png` below) -- the same close, CI-overlapping "SSL looks fine here" pattern `cpu_run1` reported, still contradicted by the chained metric.

**The RandomForest / GBT limitation**: unchanged and still correctly absent -- both remain single-shot `GridSearchCV` fits with hyperparameters tuned once (now cached from whichever seed's grid search ran first across the two separate partial `--seed-list` invocations that produced seeds 3 and 4, plus the original three) and reused with only `random_state` varying, so there is still no per-epoch quantity for either to plot.

## `comparison_val_loss.png` -- Windowed Validation Loss by Model (5-seed 95% CI)

**What it shows**: the same construction as `cpu_run1`'s version, now over 5 seeds -- LSTM 0.713 [0.679, 0.747], SSL-LSTM 0.800 [0.773, 0.827], Transformer 0.365 [0.331, 0.398], SSL-Transformer 0.362 [0.343, 0.380], RandomForest 0.665 [0.661, 0.668], GBT 0.355 [0.350, 0.360]. (This figure's title previously read "3-seed" regardless of which run label it was built for -- a hardcoded string in `visualize_ablation.py::plot_val_loss_comparison()` missed when the script was generalized to accept `--run-label`. Fixed to read the actual seed count dynamically, and both this figure and `cpu_run1`'s copy were regenerated from the fixed code so neither is a leftover from the bug.)

**What it means, next to `ablation_matrix.png`'s chained metric**: the same windowed-vs-chained disagreement `cpu_run1` reported for the Transformer family holds at 5 seeds, essentially unchanged in magnitude -- SSL-Transformer's windowed loss (0.362) is still marginally better than the from-scratch Transformer's (0.365), while the chained metric still says the opposite, more emphatically now that it's statistically cleaner (9.10 m vs. 13.99 m, no longer CI-overlapping with RandomForest or LSTM on either side, per `runs/ablation/cpu_5seeds/ANALYSIS.md`). The LSTM-family and tree-family rankings still agree directionally across both metrics, exactly as before. RandomForest's windowed number (0.665) is now the tightest CI of any bar in the figure by a wide margin (essentially invisible error bars compared to LSTM's or SSL-LSTM's) -- the windowed-loss counterpart to the chained metric's own reliability finding: this model's fitting process is close to deterministic given the data, whichever axis you measure it on.

## `trajectory_3d.png` / `trajectory_3d_top_performers.png`, `trajectory_xy.png` / `trajectory_xy_top_performers.png`, `trajectory_xz.png`

**What they show**: the same real 12.5 s held-out spiral maneuver, same 8 methods, same solid-vs-dotted (learned vs. classical) convention as `cpu_run1`'s versions -- necessarily built from this run's own representative seeds, so the six learned paths are not pixel-identical to `cpu_run1`'s figures even where the representative seed happens to be the same model family's typical behavior.

**What changed, concretely**: the qualitative story is the same as `cpu_run1/figures/ANALYSIS.md` reported -- both classical baselines launch away from the real spiral into a completely different region of space (`trajectory_xy.png`: Pure Inertial still ends off in the +80/+90 m region), all six learned models stay in the same general volume as ground truth, altitude remains the "solved" axis for every method (`trajectory_xz.png`: roughly 8-20 m band for all six learned models and the EKF, only Pure Inertial climbs unbounded past 60 m). The Transformer's representative path (still seed 2, unchanged from `cpu_run1`) is visually the closest match to the ground-truth spiral's shape in `trajectory_xy.png`, extending furthest into the loop's southward extent among the six learned models, consistent with it having both the best point estimate and, per `runs/ablation/cpu_5seeds/ANALYSIS.md`, a now-*proven* (non-overlapping-CI) edge over four of the other five arms rather than just the best guess. The top-performers reduced views (Transformer, GBT, SSL-Transformer + both baselines) again exist specifically to make that comparison legible without the other three learned paths and both baselines' excursions competing for attention in the same frame.

## `error_over_time.png` -- Position Error Growth over Time (all 8 methods)

**What it shows**: identical construction to `cpu_run1`'s version, over the same real segment (so the underlying ground-truth difficulty structure is identical by construction), using this run's representative seeds.

**What it means**: the shared-structure finding `cpu_run1/figures/ANALYSIS.md` reported reproduces exactly -- a small error bump near 1 s, a dip back toward zero around 4-4.5 s (all six learned curves happen to re-cross the true position at close to the same moment), then a shared, steep ramp starting around 5.5-6.5 s that produces most of every model's final error, before partially recovering after roughly 9 s. This is now visible on a *different* set of six representative seeds than `cpu_run1` used (four of six representative seeds changed, per the table above) and the shared-ramp shape still holds regardless -- stronger evidence than `cpu_run1` alone could offer that this is a genuine property of a harder stretch of the real maneuver affecting every architecture similarly, not an artifact of which particular seed happened to be picked as representative. Final-time ordering (Transformer lowest at ~7 m, then SSL-Transformer ~13 m, GBT ~18 m, RandomForest ~26 m, LSTM ~29 m, SSL-LSTM ~33 m, highest) matches `runs/ablation/cpu_5seeds/ANALYSIS.md`'s point-estimate ranking exactly, as it must -- these are that table's own representative-seed numbers, now visible as full time series.

## Connecting back to `runs/ablation/cpu_5seeds/ANALYSIS.md`: what these figures add

- `train_lstm.png` is the direct visual origin of the production-checkpoint story: it is what first made seed 4's unusual (never-early-stopped) training dynamics visible, motivating `ai_backend/continue_training_best_lstm.py`'s direct test of whether it had really converged.
- `train_transformer.png`'s newly-visible seed-3 upturn is the direct visual match to the windowed-vs-chained decoupling finding for that specific seed -- a concrete instance, not just an aggregate statistic, of "a training curve that looks fine mid-training can still end up producing this run's worst chained result."
- `comparison_val_loss.png` next to `ablation_matrix.png` reproduces the Transformer-family ranking reversal with a statistically cleaner gap than `cpu_run1` could show, and the title-string bug this document's own review caught and fixed is itself a small, concrete instance of this project's general "verify, don't assume" convention applied to the tooling rather than the science.
- `error_over_time.png`'s shared error-ramp structure reproducing on an almost-entirely-different set of representative seeds is stronger evidence for a genuine data-difficulty effect (not a per-seed artifact) than the single 3-seed instance of this figure could offer on its own.

## What remains a limitation, stated plainly

- Same limitations as `cpu_run1/figures/ANALYSIS.md`: no training-curve figures for RandomForest/GBT (still correctly absent, not a gap), and the representative-seed trajectory figures still show one seed's path per model, not a substitute for the CI-based statistical claims in `runs/ablation/cpu_5seeds/ANALYSIS.md`.
- Four of six representative seeds differ from `cpu_run1`'s figure set -- a reader comparing the two folders' trajectory figures side by side should not read any single-path difference between them as a finding in itself; it is a mechanical consequence of the median rule applied to a different, larger sample, already accounted for in the CI tables both documents point to.
- As `runs/ablation/cpu_5seeds/ANALYSIS.md` itself states, 5 seeds is more evidence than 3 but still not enough to fully pin down the more variable models' (Transformer, SSL-Transformer) true means with high confidence -- the same caution applies to reading any single representative-seed figure in this folder as definitive.
